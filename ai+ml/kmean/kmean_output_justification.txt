k=3: best silhouette but

It put 2119 facilities into one giant “large facility in a busy market” bucket.
That cluster is too broad to label confidently with one name.


k=4:
It separates residential facilities into four pretty distinct patterns:

Small providers in medium/large markets, low share.
Large providers in large busy markets, low share.
Mid/large providers that dominate small local markets.
Very small providers with near-zero local demand.
That is much easier to label consistently, for example:

cluster 1: Constrained by Market
cluster 0: Constrained by Size
cluster 2: Potentially Available
cluster 3: Likely Available

k=5: 
It further splits the broad market-constrained group into:

Large providers in busy markets
Mid-sized providers in busy markets
Dominant providers in small markets
Small providers in lighter markets
Zero-demand dominant providers
That gives a very natural 5-label style:

Likely Available
Potentially Available
Constrained by Market
Constrained by Size
Highly Constrained
The downside is that k=5 has a noticeably worse silhouette than k=4, so it is more interpretive than statistically strong.
